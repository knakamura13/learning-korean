# Lessons must be labs, not articles — and retention needs a scheduler

Kyle rejected the first build of Lesson 01 with: *"I want to learn interactively, not
by reading a blog-post styled tutorial."* The lesson had taught the content in prose
and appended drills; he wanted the interaction to *be* the teaching. In the same
session he asked for spaced repetition "to really master every Korean letter, sound,
and compound letter/sound."

Read together, these two pieces of feedback are one coherent position, and it changes
how every future session is built: **he wants to acquire skills by doing and retain
them by scheduling — not to consume explanations.**

## Implications

- **Every lesson is a lab.** Single-track cards, one action each, explanation only as
  feedback on what was just done. If a card reads fine without being touched, cut it.
  Recorded as a hard rule in [[NOTES.md]].
- **Prefer discovery to exposition.** Lab 01 makes him find ㅁ by saying *mmm* and
  clicking his own lips, and derive "stroke = aspiration" by feeling the puff on his
  palm — rather than being told either. Where a rule can be derived, derive it.
- **Reference documents are exempt** and should stay prose. The objection was to
  lessons that read like articles; the reference sheet has a different job (scanning)
  and he did not object to it. *(That sheet was `reference/hangul-complete.html` at
  the time; it is now the `/reference` route, generated from the domain.)*
- **Retention is now a separate system from teaching.** Labs unlock; the SRS deck
  retains. This splits the two jobs cleanly and means lessons no longer need to carry
  review load — freeing the 10-minute budget for new skill acquisition.
- **The deck is the diagnostic.** `SRS.weakest()` surfaces the cards with the most
  lapses, so future labs can target actual weak points instead of guessed ones. Ask
  the deck before designing the next lesson.

## Evidence

Not a demonstration of Korean knowledge — this is a recorded preference about
*method*, stated directly and unprompted, twice in one session. Treat it as settled
unless he says otherwise.
