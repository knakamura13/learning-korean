# Starting point: a few Hangul letters, aiming at a long-term foundation

Kyle self-reports knowing "a few characters" — some letters recognisable, but he
cannot reliably decode an unfamiliar syllable block. This is the floor: lessons may
assume Hangul is not visually alien, but must assume no letter is reliably known.

The mission has two halves, established 2026-08-05: a **correct long-term
foundation** (Hangul as step one of a multi-year path, explicitly wanting no bad
habits baked in) and **the Korean speakers in his life** (reading names and messages
from real people). The second half is what makes this concrete rather than academic,
and it sets a hard downstream dependency — reading Korean names requires batchim
(김, 박, 정, 강 all end in a final consonant), so batchim cannot be deferred as an
"advanced" topic. It is on the critical path to the thing he actually wants.

## Implications

- **Pace can be brisk.** He built an 850-card JLPT N5 deck for Japanese, so learning
  a non-Latin script is a known quantity and script anxiety is not a factor. Do not
  pad lessons with reassurance.
- **System-first, always.** He chose "explain why Hangul is built this way" over rote
  drilling. Explanations must lead with mechanism and cite it; unsupported claims
  will cost trust.
- **10-minute ceiling is real.** Every lesson is one sitting or it does not happen.
- **Prioritise batchim earlier than a standard curriculum would**, because of the
  names dependency above.
