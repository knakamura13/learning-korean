# Mission: Korean (Hangul first, then the language)

## Why

Kyle wants to build a correct, bad-habit-free foundation for a multi-year path into
Korean, and to be able to read what the Korean speakers in his life actually write —
names, messages, and the everyday text they send him. Hangul is step one, and getting
it right the first time is the point: a shaky script foundation compounds into years
of mispronunciation and slow reading.

## Success looks like

- Reads any Hangul syllable block cold — including compound vowels, double
  consonants, and clustered final consonants — without sounding it out letter by
  letter.
- Decoding is automatic rather than effortful: under ~2 seconds per unfamiliar
  syllable, so working memory is free for meaning instead of spelling.
- Can predict what a written word will *sound* like, applying liaison, nasalization,
  tensification, and aspiration rather than being surprised by them.
- Can read a Korean name or a short message from someone he knows and pronounce it
  close enough to be understood.
- Writes Hangul by hand legibly enough to be read — a stated secondary interest,
  not the primary axis.

## Constraints

- **~10 minutes per session, most days.** Every lesson must be completable in one
  sitting. Long lessons will not get done.
- **Bursty availability is likely** despite the daily intent — lessons should be
  self-contained and open with a short retrieval warm-up so gaps do not hurt.
- **Lessons must be interactive, never expository.** Stated directly: no blog-post
  style tutorials. Each lesson is a lab of action-cards where explanation arrives only
  as feedback on what he just did. Reference sheets may stay prose. See
  [learning record 0002](learning-records/0002-lessons-must-be-interactive.md).
- Prefers to understand the *system* rather than memorize items, and to *derive* the
  rule rather than be handed it.
- Wants timed drills and real text, not passive review. Recognition-only exercises
  are a waste of his session.
- **Retention is scheduled, not hoped for.** A spaced-repetition deck covering the
  full inventory (the `/review` page) is the retention mechanism; labs only unlock into it.
- Has learned a non-Latin script before (JLPT N5 Japanese, self-built flashcard
  decks), so script anxiety is not the obstacle — pace can be brisk.

## Out of scope for now

- Grammar, particles, conjugation, honorific levels. Script and pronunciation first.
- Vocabulary building as an end in itself — chasing frequency lists or TOPIK word
  counts. **Real words as reading substrate are in scope** (updated 2026-08-22):
  the vocabulary packs exist because sound changes only live inside words, and
  because reading the names, menus, and messages of the people in Kyle's life is
  the mission's second half. Meaning comes along with the decoding, not instead
  of it, and words trickle in on their own budget so they never crowd out letters.
- TOPIK preparation. Possible eventually; not steering anything today.
- Speaking and conversation practice. Production of the tense-consonant series is
  acknowledged as a months-long project, not a lesson goal.
